League of Crowns

A new MOBA built from the ground up, with our own game technology sitting on top of Ogre3D.

WORK PROJECT
IN DEVELOPMENT
YOU CAN WEAR IT SOON!

League of Crowns is a new multiplayer online battle arena I'm working on as a full-time developer at Picarda.

One of the interesting parts of the project is that we're not just putting a game together on top of an existing engine. A large part of the technology around the game is our own.

The engine

The project is written primarily in C++, with Ogre3D providing the underlying rendering technology. On top of that we've been building our own engine and game framework around the specific needs of the game.

Ogre3D handles the lower-level rendering side, while our own code provides the systems that actually make League of Crowns a game: entity management, gameplay, resources, tools, networking and everything tying those pieces together.

Don't build an engine because you can. Build the parts of an engine that your game actually needs.

Why Ogre3D?

Ogre3D gives us a solid rendering foundation without forcing the entire architecture of the game to follow the architecture of a massive all-in-one engine.

That leaves us with a useful middle ground: we can rely on a mature renderer while keeping ownership of the higher-level technology that defines League of Crowns.

Multiplayer first

Being a MOBA means multiplayer isn't an extra feature bolted onto the game later. It affects almost every important system.

Gameplay has to behave predictably across machines, state needs to be synchronized, and the client can't simply assume that everything it sees is authoritative.

That makes seemingly simple gameplay features considerably more interesting once networking enters the picture.

Tools matter

Another big part of the project is the tooling around the game. Making the actual game is only one part of making a game.

Good editors, debugging tools, resource pipelines and development utilities make it possible to iterate quickly without turning every small change into an engineering project.

Still very much in progress

League of Crowns is still being developed, so the technology and the game itself are constantly changing. Systems get rewritten, prototypes get thrown away, and occasionally something works perfectly until someone asks why.

That's also what makes it interesting to work on. There's a lot of room to experiment with architecture, performance and gameplay while building something that eventually has to survive real players.
